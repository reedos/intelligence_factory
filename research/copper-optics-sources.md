# Watts to Tokens — Copper & Optics Interconnect Fact Sheet

Sourced figures for the physical medium that carries a bit: passive copper (DAC), active
copper (ACC, AEC), and the optics ladder (DSP pluggables, LPO/LRO, CPO, coherent). Compiled
2026-09-27 from a research pass followed by an independent verification pass — every fetched
page was re-fetched or cross-checked before a fact was marked confirmed. Basis labels match
`power-chain-sources.md`: **Published spec** (a vendor or standards body states it),
**Industry typical** (trade press or reference designs agree), **Estimate** (derived here, or
sources conflict). This sheet does not repeat link rates or switch specs already in
`interconnect-sources.md`, or fiber/connector counts already in `interconnect-physical-sources.md`
— both were read first and are cited, not re-derived, except where this pass found a correction
(flagged inline and in "Conflicts with existing research," below).

Only claims that survived independent verification (confirmed, or confirmed-with-correction)
appear in the tables. Everything sought but not confirmed is in "Not used," at the end, with
the reason.

### (c) Five plain sentences, each tied to a sourced row above

1. Copper is nearly free bandwidth if the two ends are close enough: at 100 Gb/s per lane, a plain copper cable reaches at least 2 meters before the signal gets too weak to read reliably, at essentially no power cost. *(Section 1, reach at 100 Gb/s/lane)*
2. Push the same wire to carry twice the data per lane, and its reach falls by more than half: at 200 Gb/s per lane, passive copper drops to somewhere under a meter and a few meters at most — not far enough to reliably leave a single rack. *(Section 1, reach at 200 Gb/s/lane; IEEE's own 1 m objective)*
3. The industry's two ways to stretch copper past that point both add power at the connector, not in the cable itself: a simple redriver chip pushes copper out to about 3 meters for a couple of watts (active copper cable, ACC); a full signal-regenerating chip pushes it out to 7–9 meters for roughly ten times the power (active electrical cable, AEC). *(Section 2, ACC vs. AEC power at 200 Gb/s/lane; AEC reach ceiling)*
4. Past about 10 meters, or once a link has to leave its rack's immediate neighbors, the industry switches to optics regardless of the extra cost and power — no copper design gets there affordably anymore. *(Section 4, copper/optics crossover rule)*
5. Even a modern optical module spends real power just to convert an electrical signal to light and back — the way engineers cut that cost is by trimming or removing the module's own signal-processing chip (its DSP): linear optics (LPO) with no DSP at all can use roughly half the power of a fully-retimed pluggable, and co-packaged optics (CPO) goes further still by building the optics into the switch chip's own package. *(Section 3, Semtech's power ladder; Broadcom Bailly/TH6 CPO power)*
