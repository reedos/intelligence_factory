# The Intelligence Factory — "Inside the Optics" Fact Sheet

Sourced figures for a new millimeter-scale level: (A) a 1.6T OSFP pluggable module, DSP-based
(DR8 / 2×DR4 style) and its LPO variant, and (B) an NVIDIA Spectrum-X / Quantum-X Photonics
style co-packaged optics (CPO) switch package. Compiled 09/28/2026. Read first, and not
re-derived except where noted: `research/copper-optics-sources.md` (power ladder, LPO/LRO,
CPO reliability, NVIDIA laser-ratio reconciliation), `research/interconnect-sources.md`, and
`research/interconnect-physical-sources.md` (fiber/connector counts, NVIDIA Quantum-X
Photonics chiplet count already sourced there). `src/sources.js` ids are reused where the
same citation already exists (`nvidia-spectrum-x-cpo`, `nvidia-cpo-industry-collaboration-blog`,
`storagereview-nvidia-cpo-production`, `broadcom-davisson-cpo`, `nextplatform-broadcom-cpo`) —
this file does not re-quote those, only points to the section that already covers them and
adds what those sections don't.

Basis labels match the rest of the site: **Published spec** (a vendor, MSA or standards body
states it), **Industry typical** (trade press/reference designs agree), **Estimate** (derived
here or sources conflict), and **Vendor claim** used explicitly wherever a figure is one
company's own marketing number, not an independent measurement.

Note on tooling this pass: `osfpmsa.org`'s PDFs (the actual OSFP MSA mechanical spec, Rev 5.0
and 5.1) and the IEEE EPS "Linear Pluggable Optics" PDF both downloaded but returned only raw
PDF binary stream to this session's fetch tool — the same PDF-rendering limitation noted in
`copper-optics-sources.md`. Figures attributed to the MSA below therefore come from vendor/trade
pages that state numbers as drawn from the MSA, not from a direct read of the MSA text itself;
flagged per-row. `marvell.com` returned HTTP 403 on every direct fetch, consistent with the
copper-optics pass — Marvell figures below are press-release (PRNewswire/GlobeNewswire mirrors)
or trade-press only.

## C. Transmit and receive chain (lasers, modulators, drivers, photodiodes, TIAs)

| Item | Value | Basis | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Laser type by reach class | Directly modulated lasers (DMLs, distributed-feedback/DFB structure) used for shorter-reach single-mode optics such as DR8 (≤500 m). Electro-absorption modulated lasers (EMLs) used for 1.6T/800G optics with reach ≥10 km | Published spec | [Juniper, "Know Your 1.6T Transceiver"](https://www.juniper.net/documentation/us/en/hardware/1.6t-optics-cables-guide/optics-1.6t/topics/concept/1.6t-know-your-transceiver.html) — "DMLs are used for single-mode optics such as DR8"; "High speed optics such as 1.6T and 800G, with a reach of 10km or greater, generally use EMLs" |
| Receive chain, per-lane component count (8-lane, 1.6T module) | 8× photodetectors (photodiodes) and 8× transimpedance amplifiers (TIAs), one pair per optical lane — the TIA "converts and amplifies the electrical current from the photodiode into an electrical voltage level" | Published spec | Same Juniper page |
| Silicon photonics (SiPh) modulator types, general | Three modulator families used in SiPh transceivers: MZI/Mach-Zehnder interferometer (mature, high-bandwidth, the current commercial mainstream), MRR/micro-ring resonator (ultra-compact footprint, but temperature-sensitive), and EAM via SiGe heterogeneous integration (fast, compact) | Industry typical | [tspasemiconductor.substack.com, "OFC Preview: The Race to 400G per Lane"](https://tspasemiconductor.substack.com/p/ofc-preview-the-race-to-400g-per-lane) (trade technical newsletter, citing published OFC-adjacent research) |
| SiPh requires an external light source | Silicon does not natively emit light efficiently — SiPh transceivers require a separate III-V (typically InP) laser source coupled into the silicon waveguide, unlike EML/DML approaches where the laser is integrated into the same III-V chip as the modulator | Industry typical | Same source |
| Single-lane 200G SiPh demonstrations | Single-lane rates of 200 Gb/s (80 Gbaud, PAM-6) and 176 Gb/s (88 Gbaud, PAM-4) demonstrated on conventional silicon-photonic Mach-Zehnder modulators, in the research literature | Industry typical (research demonstration, not a shipping product) | Same source; [Optica, "Up to single lane 200G optical interconnects with silicon photonic modulator," OFC 2019 Th4A.6](https://opg.optica.org/abstract.cfm?uri=ofc-2019-Th4A.6) |
| 90 GHz silicon MZM for 1.6 Tbps (200G/λ) IMDD | A 90 GHz-bandwidth silicon Mach-Zehnder modulator with an integrated electronic equalizer, targeted at 1.6 Tbps (200 Gb/s per wavelength) intensity-modulation/direct-detection (IMDD) transceivers — a research paper, not a named shipping part | Industry typical (peer-reviewed conference paper) | [Optica, OFC 2025 M2K.1, "90 GHz Silicon Mach-Zehnder Modulator with Integrated Equalizer for 1.6 Tbps (200G/λ) IMDD Transceivers"](https://opg.optica.org/abstract.cfm?uri=OFC-2025-M2K.1) |
| TFLN (thin-film lithium niobate) modulators | Strong electro-optic (Pockels) effect gives TFLN modulators >100 GHz bandwidth and very low drive voltage — well beyond what 200G/lane datacenter IMDD links need. | Component-level power figures (TIA/driver/DSP, pJ/bit) | Trade-derived figures circulating for 1.6T-class designs: TIA+driver combined ≈4 pJ/bit (≈13 W total across 8 lanes' worth); TIA alone ≈1.6 pJ/bit (≈2.6 W/chip); DSP chip alone ≈5 pJ/bit (≈16 W). ## E. LPO (linear pluggable optics) variant

| Item | Value | Basis | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| What LPO removes | The module's own DSP — no retiming, no CDR, no onboard FEC/equalization chip in the signal path | Published spec | [Semtech, "AI Data Center Basics: What Is Linear Pluggable Optics?"](https://blog.semtech.com/ai-data-center-basics-what-is-linear-pluggable-optics-lpo) — already cited in `research/copper-optics-sources.md` |
| What LPO keeps | Transmit side: a continuous-time linear equalizer (CTLE) for input conditioning, a linear driver, and the modulator (laser). Receive side: a photodiode, a TIA, and potentially a linear equalizer — all linear/analog, none of them digitally retiming the signal | Published spec | [Flexoptix, "Introducing Linear Pluggable Optics (LPO)"](https://www.flexoptix.net/en/blog/blog/introducing-linear-pluggable-optics) |
| Host requirements | The host ASIC's own SerDes takes over the sophisticated equalization (and some FEC) work the module's DSP used to do; the LPO MSA specifies hosts must implement RS(544,514) FEC, and module and host exchange insertion-loss/CTLE calibration data over the CMIS "Versatile Control Set" so the host's linear equalizer can track the real, port-specific channel loss | Published spec | Same Flexoptix piece |
| LPO MSA status | First specification (100 Gb/s/lane, up to 800GbE) completed 3/31/2025; a 200 Gb/s/lane follow-on was in development as of the copper-optics pass | Published spec | Already sourced in `research/copper-optics-sources.md`, Section 3 ([Lightwave Online](https://www.lightwaveonline.com/home/article/55278631/lpo-msa-releases-linear-pluggable-optical-modules-specification)) — reused, not re-derived |
| LPO power figures | Semtech's own 200 Gb/s/lane power ladder: fully-retimed (DSP) ≈23–25 W (this is specifically the 1.6T DR8 class, per the copper-optics pass's correction) → ~20 W; RTLR/LRO ≈16 W today → 14–15 W; LPO ≈10 W target — Semtech does not state a port capacity (800G vs. 1.6T) for the LRO/LPO figures | Published spec | Already sourced in `research/copper-optics-sources.md`, Section 3 — reused |
| Marvell 1.6T LPO chipset | Marvell announced a 1.6 Tb/s LPO chipset for "optical short-reach, scale-up compute fabric interconnects" — press-release title and existence confirmed via search; the release page itself returned HTTP 403 on direct fetch, so no further figures (power, part number, date) could be independently pulled this pass | Published spec (existence only) | [Marvell newsroom, "Marvell Introduces 1.6 Tbps LPO Chipset"](https://www.marvell.com/company/newsroom/marvell-introduces-1-6-tbps-lpo-chipset.html) — title/URL only; content not independently fetched (403) |
| LPO adoption status | 800G LPO reached early commercial deployment in 2026, with Meta and Amazon reported (trade-sourced, not vendor-confirmed) placing orders; at 1.6T, thermal/power headroom pushes the industry toward LRO rather than full LPO for now | Industry typical | Already sourced in `research/copper-optics-sources.md`, Section 3 — reused |

- **Pluggable-to-CPO signal loss, 20 dB vs. 22 dB.** Lambda's independent Q3450-LD unboxing (6/1/2026) states "roughly 20dB to 4dB." NVIDIA's own developer blog, "Scaling AI Factories with Co-Packaged Optics," states "22 dB" to "approximately 4 dB." - **NVIDIA's efficiency-claim number itself changed over 2025–2026** (already flagged in `research/copper-optics-sources.md`, reused here for completeness): the original March 2025 announcement claimed "3.5× power efficiency" vs. pluggables; by the August 2026 full-production update this had become "5× lower power." ## For the page

A short synthesis, each line tied to a sourced row above, for whoever writes the level's card copy:

1. A 1.6T OSFP module is a small, dense electronics package: roughly 108 mm long, 22.6 mm wide, 13 mm tall including its finned heat sink, carrying 8 electrical lanes at 200+ Gb/s each in through a 60-pin edge connector, and sending the same 8 channels out as light through one or two MPO fiber connectors. *(Section A)*
2. Almost everything that makes the module "smart" lives in one chip, the DSP: it cleans up the incoming electrical signal, converts it to light through laser drivers, and does the reverse on the way back in — modern 3 nm DSPs from Marvell (Ara) and Broadcom (Sian3) hold total module power under roughly 23 W at 1.6T, a real improvement over the prior generation. *(Section B)*
3. Depending on reach, the light itself comes from two different kinds of laser: a directly modulated laser for short single-mode links, or a separate externally modulated laser (EML) for links of 10 km or more — and a growing share of modules instead use silicon photonics, where the laser is a separate chip whose light is coupled into a silicon modulator on the transceiver's own die. *(Section C)*
4. LPO strips the module down to its analog essentials — no DSP, no retiming — pushing that signal-cleanup work onto the switch chip itself; it can cut a module's power by roughly half, but only works over shorter, cleaner electrical and optical paths than a fully-retimed module can tolerate. *(Section E)*
5. NVIDIA's co-packaged optics goes further than either: instead of a pluggable module at all, 18 tiny optical engines are packaged directly onto the switch chip itself, stacked chip-on-chip with TSMC's COUPE process, fed by swappable external laser modules at the front panel — collapsing a signal path that used to lose ~20+ dB down to about 4 dB, and NVIDIA's own claim is up to roughly a 3–5× cut in power per port versus a pluggable module doing the same job. *(Section F)*
