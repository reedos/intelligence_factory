2.2 · Voltage

Volts down, amps up

Power is volts times amps. Each step down in voltage raises the current a conductor has to carry, which is why the last step happens millimeters from the chip.

03Data

Copper inside the rack, light across the hall, fiber between campuses: how fast each link is, what carries it, and how a model is split to fit.

3.1 · Bandwidth

Bandwidth down, latency up

The data path has its own staircase. The closer two pieces of silicon sit, the faster they can talk. That is why a model is split the way it is: the chattiest work stays inside a rack on NVLink, less of it crosses the hall, and as little as possible crosses the country.

3.2 · Links

The links, drawn and counted

Three networks, three physical media. Copper ties 72 GPUs into one machine inside a rack; single-mode fiber and pluggable optics tie racks into a campus fabric; coherent optics on leased fiber tie campuses together. Counts are for this 100 MW campus and one common fabric layout, so treat them as estimates of scale, not a bill of materials.

Copper and optics

The media ladder

Every link in this campus is one of a few physical media, and each medium trades reach for power. Passive copper costs nothing to add but runs out of room fast; every step past it puts a chip in the path to buy distance back.

Inside the module

What LPO and CPO change

An 800G optical module is a small assembly line: a connector, a chip that cleans up the signal, a laser that turns it into light, and a receiver that turns light back into a signal. LPO saves power by removing that signal-processing chip outright, so the host's own SerDes drives and reads the line directly. CPO saves power a different way, by shortening the electrical run between the switch chip and the optics and cutting out the pluggable module and its connector — not necessarily by deleting the signal-processing chip too.

3.3 · Parallelism

How a model is split

The bandwidth staircase decides the layout. The chattiest kind of splitting goes where the links are fastest, and each step outward carries less, less often. Switch the 3D view to Data and open the hall to see one layout drawn on the rack tops.

04Heat

Every watt ends as heat. The path from a GPU die to the outside air, and what it costs in power and water.

4.1 · Temperature

Hot to cold

Every watt ends as heat, and heat only flows downhill. Each hop from die to outdoor air costs a few degrees, which is why warmer water is the trick: NVIDIA's newest spec feeds racks at 45 °C, so even a 35 °C afternoon can take the heat through dry coolers, without chillers.
